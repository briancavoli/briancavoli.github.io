Experience & Results / Growth Systems

From Marketing Programs to Growth Systems

Building a more systematic approach to business growth.

At Tribute Home Care, I built a centralized marketing function from the ground up. Over time, that work evolved well beyond brand building, demand generation, and marketing communications.

I saw opportunities to improve how the business developed referral relationships, converted inquiries into clients, managed pricing, and recruited caregivers. Each required more than a new campaign or technology tool. They required better processes, stronger coordination, new capabilities, and clearer visibility into performance.

By late 2025, I brought these approaches together into what I called Growth Systems.

A growth system isn’t a tool, a workflow, or a marketing initiative. It’s a repeatable way of operating that improves performance and can scale across people, teams, and markets.

The Challenge: Moving Beyond Good Intentions

Home care is an operationally intensive business, with dedicated people working hard to deliver excellent care. But good intentions and hard work don’t automatically produce effective growth practices.

Referral relationships can be reactive rather than strategic. Sales teams can focus on responding to inquiries rather than helping families make informed decisions. Pricing decisions can be disconnected from broader financial goals. Recruiting can emphasize applicant volume over the complete candidate journey.

The challenge was to connect strategy, people, processes, technology, and measurement so the organization could consistently improve performance.

Five common elements

Building the System

I developed a common structure that could be applied to different business challenges. Each Growth System brings together five elements:

  • Strategy

    Identify the business opportunity, set priorities, and define what success looks like.

  • Tools

    Provide the marketing assets, technology, information, and resources people need.

  • Process

    Define how work gets done, who is responsible, and how capabilities are adopted.

  • Measurement

    Establish clear performance indicators and make progress visible.

  • Insights

    Use data, customer feedback, and increasingly AI to understand what’s working, identify opportunities, and guide improvements.

In practice, every system draws on the same ingredients:

People and teamsBusiness processes and best practicesCRM and existing technologyData, analytics, and AILeadership accountabilityContinuous learning and improvement

AI makes these systems faster and smarter. It isn’t the system itself.

Implementation and measurement during 2026

Four Growth Priorities. Measurable Progress.

The approach was developed in late 2025 around agreed-upon business priorities, then implemented and measured during 2026. Each system follows the same logic: business opportunity, strategic approach, implementation and adoption, and measurement and improvement.

Systems marked Measured were implemented and tracked against prior-year results. Areas marked Framework are designed but not yet measured, and I don’t present them as results.

01
Measured · 2026

Strategic Partner Development

From transactional referrals to long-term account growth.

Business opportunity
Referral relationships were largely reactive. Analysis showed it could take nine to ten months for a relationship to become a meaningful revenue source.
Strategic approach
Account segmentation, stakeholder development, differentiated marketing, and account planning focused on the full potential of each partner.
Implementation and adoption
Partner account plans and CRM-based performance intelligence guided where the sales team invested its time.
Measurement and improvement
Partner revenue, referrals, and long-hour opportunities tracked year over year, with early visibility into relationships in decline.
2026 results, through Q3
  • +18% year-over-year revenue among the top 12 partners.
  • Partners with declining referrals fell from roughly 8–9 a year to four.
  • Next-tier partners (#13–24): revenue +24%, referrals +21%, long-hour opportunities +85%, long-hour wins +77%.
02
Measured · 2026

Client Sales Conversion

From passive inquiry handling to consultative sales.

Business opportunity
Long-hour conversion had been flat for about three years, and families often didn’t know which questions to ask.
Strategic approach
Sales training, messaging, follow-up tools, and CRM workflows to help teams guide families through an unfamiliar and often emotional decision.
Implementation and adoption
AI-assisted analysis of actual conversations and CRM notes showed what families needed most: certainty about value, availability, caregiver fit, and our ability to deliver. Those findings became coaching, training, and new sales communications.
Measurement and improvement
Conversion reporting tracked against prior years to see whether the changes held.
2026 result, through Q3
  • An 8% relative improvement in long-hour sales conversion, following roughly three years of flat performance.
03
Measured · 2026

Pricing & Revenue Performance

From individual pricing decisions to consistency and accountability.

Business opportunity
Pricing decisions were made case by case and could drift from financial goals, especially in competitive markets.
Strategic approach
A systematic approach informed by competitive conditions, customer behavior, discount practices, and actual billing performance.
Implementation and adoption
Regular analysis and review helped teams understand pricing decisions and their impact on financial performance.
Measurement and improvement
Billing rates tracked monthly against budget, by region.
2026 result
  • Cut a region’s billing-rate gap to budget by more than half in about six months.
04
Measured · 2026

Caregiver Recruiting

From recruiting campaigns to a stronger candidate and referral experience.

Business opportunity
Recruiting focused on generating applicants, with less attention to the full candidate journey.
Strategic approach
Employment branding, recruiting communications, and caregiver stories designed to generate more qualified opportunities.
Implementation and adoption
The Share the Care referral campaigns engaged current caregivers in recruiting the next ones.
Measurement and improvement
Referral volume, hires, and cost per hire tracked by campaign.
2026 results
  • Caregiver referral volume doubled, with roughly three times as many hires from the campaigns, at a significantly lower cost per hire.
Framework · not yet measured

Connecting the Full Client Experience

The broader Growth Systems framework also includes Client Experience & Reputation: listening to clients, responding to feedback, strengthening relationships, generating reviews, and creating opportunities for advocacy and re-engagement.

It connects client experiences back to the organization’s reputation and future growth. Together, the five areas show how growth activities can reinforce one another rather than operate as disconnected programs.

How the systems learn

From Data to Insight to Action

Early in my tenure, much of the information needed to understand sales and partner performance simply wasn’t available in a useful form. I introduced CRM infrastructure and reporting that made activities, relationships, and outcomes visible.

Over time, we moved from measuring what happened to understanding why it happened and what we could do differently. AI accelerated that, letting us examine customer conversations and CRM notes in ways traditional reporting couldn’t.

But the real value came after the analysis: changing messaging, training people differently, improving workflows, and measuring what happened next.

  1. Datacreates visibility.
  2. Insightscreate understanding.
  3. Actioncreates results.

What Growth Systems Demonstrates

Growth Systems is the latest form of an approach I’ve used throughout my career: building capabilities that help organizations grow and keep improving. It takes marketing strategy, customer understanding, creative thinking, technology, data, and cross-functional leadership, along with the patience to help people adopt new ways of working.

The objective isn’t more complexity or another layer of management. It’s to make better performance intentional, measurable, and repeatable.

Performance figures reflect results during implementation of these initiatives, through Q3 2026 unless noted. They represent observed business improvements rather than outcomes attributable exclusively to marketing or any single system.
